# The Honest Woodcutter and the Golden Axe

## Prompt / Incomplete Story Beginning
*Once upon a time, there lived a poor but honest woodcutter in a tranquil village bordering a deep forest. Every morning, he went into the woods to fell dry branches...*

## Model Story

Once upon a time, there lived a poor but exceptionally honest woodcutter in a peaceful village bordering a deep forest. Every morning, he went into the woods with his worn iron axe to fell dry branches. By selling this firewood in the local marketplace, he earned a modest livelihood to support his family.

One sultry afternoon, as he was cutting a branch dangling precariously over a deep, swift-flowing river, his iron axe slipped accidentally from his sweaty palms and plunged into the churning water. The water was deep and turbulent, and the poor woodcutter could not swim. Deprived of his sole instrument of survival, he sat despairingly on the grassy riverbank and wept bitter tears of helplessness.

Moved by his genuine lamentation, a luminous water spirit (Jol-Devi) emerged from the river depths. "Why are you weeping so sorrowfully, my good man?" she inquired gently. The woodcutter explained his misfortune. 

Diving into the river, the spirit re-emerged holding an axe cast from glittering, pure gold. "Is this splendid axe yours?" she asked. The honest woodcutter shook his head humbly and said, "No, respected spirit, this golden axe does not belong to me."

The spirit plunged into the depths a second time and brought out an axe fashioned from shining silver. The woodcutter gazed at it and politely replied, "No, madam, this silver axe is not mine either. I am merely a humble woodcutter."

Diving a third time, the spirit retrieved the old, rusted iron axe with its chipped wooden handle. Seeing his familiar tool, the woodcutter leapt up in unfeigned joy and exclaimed, "Yes! That is my axe! Thank you from the bottom of my heart!"

The water spirit was profoundly pleased by the man's unshakeable honesty and lack of greed. "Your truthfulness has touched my heart," she said smilingly. "Keep your iron axe, and accept both the golden and silver axes as gifts of virtue." The woodcutter returned home overjoyed, forever relieved of his acute poverty.

## Moral of the Story
**Honesty is the best policy and never goes unrewarded.**

## Writing Tips
- Use evocative adjectives to create vivid imagery (*sultry afternoon*, *plunged*, *turbulent*, *unfeigned joy*).
- Clearly state the moral lesson at the conclusion of any story writing prompt.
